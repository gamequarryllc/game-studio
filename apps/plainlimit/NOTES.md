# Plainlimit: notes

Slug `plainlimit`, bundle id `com.gamequarry.plainlimit`. Until 2026-11-11 the working name was "Screen Battery" and the folder was `apps/screen-battery/`.

## The idea (the owner's, 2026-11-11)

A visual warning that you are nearing your screen-time goal, read at a glance from the home screen by someone who would rather not open their phone. The owner chose a battery over a number: six cells that empty through the day and a colour that goes from green to red. It has to stay simple.

## What the owner signed off

1. **Setup:** one daily goal (3 hours by default) and a recharge time (6:00 AM by default). The battery's day runs from recharge to recharge, not midnight to midnight, so running out at night means staying empty until morning.
2. **The widget:** six cells, each one sixth of the goal. Colour fades green, to yellow, to red. Words instead of live numbers ("Plenty left", "Half gone", "Getting low", "Nearly out", "Empty"), with the goal as a fixed label. Small and medium for the home screen, and one without colour for the lock screen.
3. **What counts:** everything (the default), a "time-wasters" preset (Social, Games, Entertainment), or the person's own picks (Pro).
4. **Always-allowed apps:** Phone, Messages and Maps by default. They never lock.
5. **As it runs low:** one notification on the last cell; a warning screen over apps on the last cell and again shortly before the end, which can be tapped through.
6. **When it is empty,** one setting with three levels: Warn (red screen, tap through), Work for it (solve sums for more time; the default), Locked (nothing opens until the recharge except the always-allowed apps).
7. **Sums:** three for 15 minutes, then five for 10, then seven for 5. The battery stays empty; only the lock lifts.
8. **Money:** the whole core free; one pay-once Pro for the person's own picks of what counts. Not built yet.

**Pinned for later, not in this build:** earning time back with steps (Pro); groups of people who also have the app, holding each other to it (Pro, and only if Apple's rules allow a "past the goal today" flag to leave the phone). **Dropped:** texting someone as the price of more time.

## Why words and cells, not minutes

An app is never told how many minutes have been used. It can only ask the phone to speak up when a total is reached, and a widget is redrawn when the system chooses, a few dozen times a day at most. A live minute counter would visibly lag. Six cells need six updates a day, and a word like "Getting low" stays true for half an hour, so nothing on the home screen is ever caught being late.

## Look

The owner's note on the first build (2026-11-11): a good baseline, but dull. The second pass:

- **Today is the battery's colour, edge to edge.** The top of the screen is a field of the day's colour with a large battery, the word at the largest size the phone allows on one line, and the recharge time with a live "in 12 h 13 min". The rest of the app stays on one dark ground (`#0E1011`), so that colour is the only colour on any screen. No light version.
- **The colour marks what is chosen everywhere else:** the open tab, the chosen level, the goal's figures, the filled part of the day's ruler, finished sums. The deep red of an empty battery is too dark to read on the dark ground, so there it gives way to a lighter red (`accentOnDark` in `src/domain/tone.ts`, held to 4.5 to 1 by a test).
- **The battery is drawn like a sticker:** a thick casing, a hard shadow under it, a short glint on each lit cell. One drawing (`src/ui/Battery.tsx`) at every size; the icon is the Glow widget's battery instead: white casing, four green cells lit up on a dark ground (`art/make_icons.py`).
- **Movement:** a used-up cell fades and shrinks away, the colour eases to the next one over half a second, and the last cell left breathes slowly. All of it stands still when the phone is set to reduce motion (`src/ui/motion.ts`).
- **The day's ruler** under "Used so far": six stretches, one per cell, filling as the battery empties.
- Space Grotesk for the word, titles and figures, set large and tight; the system font for everything else.
- The colour of the day is worked out in OKLCH and turned into hex, and a test holds every point of the day to a text contrast of 4.5 to 1.
- The cells and the word carry the meaning as well as the colour, so it reads without colour vision.
- The screens that cover an app keep to what an iPhone allows there: a picture, a title, one line, two buttons. Once the battery is empty the main button is always "Close this app"; the way through is the smaller one.
- Sentence case, no exclamation marks, one uppercase label style (the small group labels and the goal chip).

## What is real and what is pretend

| Part | In a browser or Expo Go | In an iPhone build with Screen Time on |
|---|---|---|
| Goal, recharge time, levels | Real, saved on the phone | The same, and handed to the phone as the day's plan |
| Battery cells, colour, words | Real logic, fed by pretend use | Fed by the checkpoints the phone reports |
| Minutes used | Pretend: added by hand under Developer | A floor: the largest checkpoint reached ("At least 1h 30m") |
| Per-app times on Today | Not there | "What used it": the top five apps since the recharge, drawn by Apple's report extension (`targets/report/`). The app never sees the list |
| Always-allowed list | Names typed in | Apple's app picker; the app only learns how many are ticked |
| What counts | Everything or the preset, saved | Everything, or whatever is ticked in Apple's picker |
| Warning and empty screens | Previews opened from Developer | Drawn by the phone over other apps |
| Sums | Real | The same, reached from a notification |
| Home screen and lock screen widgets | Not there | Native, in Swift (`targets/widget/`) |
| The lock | Not there | Apple's Screen Time |
| Pro | Not built | Not built |

The Developer section (pretend use, recharge now, the two previews) shows in test builds and the web preview, never in a store build, and it hides once real Screen Time is on (`DEV_TOOLS` in `src/platform/screenTime.ts`).

## How the iPhone side is put together

**Written 2026-11-11 and compiled on the studio's Mac runners. Not one line of it has run on a real iPhone yet.** Everything below is how it is meant to work; "To check on the phone" further down is the list of what has to be seen with eyes.

- **The library.** `react-native-device-activity` 0.6.1 gives the app Screen Time permission, Apple's app picker, monitoring, and three extensions (monitor, shield action, shield configuration) that it drives from settings the app writes into the shared App Group. The app writes no Swift for those.
- **`plugins/withTargets.js`** runs on every `expo prebuild`: it copies the library's three extensions into `targets/` (not kept in git), gives the monitor the bundle id registered with Apple, adds three lines to the monitor so it redraws the widget, and removes the push-notification entitlement that the notifications package adds by default.
- **`targets/widget/`** is ours: the six-cell battery for the home screen (small, medium) and the lock screen. It reads the goal and recharge time the app left in the App Group (`plainlimit_plan`) and the times the monitor recorded for each checkpoint, and applies the same rules as the app: a checkpoint counts if it came after the last recharge and not impossibly early. A test (`monitorPlan.test.ts`) keeps its colours, words and names equal to the app's.
- **`modules/plainlimit-widgets/`** is a ten-line native module so the app can ask for a widget redraw after a setting changes.
- **`src/domain/monitorPlan.ts`** turns the settings into the day's plan as plain data. **`src/platform/screenTime.ts`** is the only file that hands it to the library.

- **`targets/report/`** is ours: Apple's usage report extension, which draws the "What used it" list on Today. The phone runs it in a sealed box and shows its picture inside the app; `modules/plainlimit-widgets/` has the view that gives it a place on screen and tells it which hours and which apps. The owner asked to see it working (2026-11-11); it is meant to become the Pro feature later. The target plugin has no type for a report extension, so the target is declared as its nearest relative ("app-intent", also an ExtensionKit extension) with its own Info.plist.

The day's plan:

| When | What the phone does |
|---|---|
| Each cell used up (`cell-1` to `cell-5`) | Records the time; the widget redraws |
| Last cell begins (`cell-5`) | Covers the counted apps with the "Last cell" screen. "Keep going" takes it away |
| A few minutes before the end (`final-warning`) | A "Nearly out" notification |
| Goal reached (`empty`) | Covers the counted apps with the empty screen for the chosen level |
| A minute before the recharge time | Takes every covering screen away |

What the empty screen's second button does: Warn takes the screen away for the rest of the day; Work for it sends a notification that opens the sums (a covering screen cannot open an app directly); Locked has no second button. After the sums are solved the app uncovers the apps until a time on the clock ("Your apps open until 7:33 PM") and asks the phone to cover them again at that moment, used or not. (The first test builds re-covered after that many minutes of *use*, which did not match what the screen promised; the owner asked for the clock.) If the app is open when the time passes, it covers them itself as well.

Decisions made while writing it, each of which the owner can change:

1. **iOS 18 or later.** The library's extensions are built for 18; an app that installed on older phones but could not count there would be worse than not installing.
2. **"Everything" counts every app, the always-allowed ones too.** Apple's counting has no "everything except". The always-allowed apps are only spared from being covered.
3. **"Time-wasters" on a phone is whatever is ticked in Apple's picker.** An app cannot pre-tick Social, Games and Entertainment; the screen asks the person to. So on a phone, Time-wasters and "your own picks" are the same thing, and there is nothing left for Pro to unlock there. Pro needs a different hook (ideas: the lock screen widget, more than one top-up a day, the pinned steps and groups).
4. **Settings lock while the battery is empty** (on a real phone only). Otherwise the way round an empty battery is to nudge the goal up.
5. **The day Screen Time is turned on counts from that moment**, not from the recharge time. (The first test build did the opposite, which with a small goal emptied the battery the instant it was turned on.) From the next recharge on, the phone is asked to keep the whole day's use, so a change to the settings does not refill the battery. On that first day only, changing a setting does restart the count.
6. **Test builds can always get out.** In a playtest build the Screen Time card has "Start the day over" (a full battery, every cover removed, counting from now) and keeps "Turn off" even while the settings are locked. Neither is in a store build.

## Known limits and risks

- **Checkpoints that arrive far too early.** On recent iOS versions the phone has reported limits as reached moments after they were set. `isPlausible` in `src/domain/checkpoints.ts` throws away a checkpoint that arrives sooner than its own minutes after the recharge. It cannot catch one that is early but possible (90 minutes reported after two hours of wall time), and it needs testing against a real phone.
- **The empty screen's button cannot open this app.** Apple gives no supported way. The supported route is a notification the person taps, so "I need more time" is two taps: the button, then the notification. Unofficial ways round it have been rejected in review.
- **"Everything counts" and always-allowed apps.** Apple has "block everything except these" for the lock but not for counting, so in the Everything mode time in Phone or Messages still drains the battery, though those apps never lock. The preset and the person's own picks avoid it. The What counts screen says so.
- **Block-everything-except has had bugs** where the exceptions were blocked too. Because the list is for emergencies, it must be tried on a real phone before anyone relies on it.
- **Widget updates are a request, not a command.** A cell can go dark a few minutes late. Six cells make that invisible; a finer colour fade between cells is a bonus if it proves reliable.
- **A second device.** Screen Time shared across devices is not handled; one iPhone only.

- **An early checkpoint can still cover apps.** The plausibility check protects what the app and the widget show. The covering itself is done by the phone the moment it reports a checkpoint, early or not. If the phone turns out to report them early, the cover actions need a guard of their own.
- **Counting "everything" rests on an assumption:** that a checkpoint registered with no apps, categories or sites counts all use of the phone. If it does not, "Everything" has to become "tick every category in Apple's picker".
- **Changing a setting re-registers the day.** The phone is asked to keep the day's count when that happens (iOS 17.4 and later can), and checkpoints already passed should simply be reported again. Unverified.

Batch 1 (build 45):

- **Finer minutes.** Checkpoints between the cells, about eighteen to a day in whole five minutes (every ten on a 3-hour day), named `m10`, `m20`... ## One list, counted from the recharge (2026-11-13, build 48)

The owner asked for the battery to follow the phone's own Screen Time for the apps that count, to install at any time of day without starting at zero, and for apps he does not count (Maps) never to drain it. Agreed on, and built:

- **One list.** What counts is whatever is ticked in Apple's picker: the usual way is to tick All Apps & Categories and untick what should not count. Ticked apps use up the battery and are the only ones covered when it is empty; unticked apps never count and never lock. The "Everything" choice and the always-allowed list are gone (they allowed apps that still counted, the thing the owner found unclear). With nothing ticked, nothing is registered, and Today says so with "Choose apps". Leaving out one app inside a ticked category means unticking the category and ticking its other apps; apps installed later in it are then not counted until ticked. Exclusion is by Apple's picker, not by counting twice and subtracting.
- **Always from the recharge.** Every registration asks the phone for the day since the recharge, so installing at 4 PM starts with the day's real use, and turning counting off and on refills nothing. (Build 47's "keep today's count" rule is gone with it.)
- **The last known figure.** The danger in counting from the recharge is iOS 26's false reports right after a day is registered. While counting works the app notes the most that can have been used by now (the next checkpoint not yet reported, plus twenty minutes since build 75; `plainlimit_known`). A report arriving in the ten minutes after the next registration is judged as if the count had begun that many minutes before the note (since the tidy-up, worked out once at registration and saved as `plainlimit_guard`), and the phone is told not to act on a checkpoint before that while the guard is open (until build 75 this applied to every later report, which threw out real late ones; see "Held back before Submit"). Re-registering mid-day after a settings change, access coming back, or a re-registration can then not lock on a false report. The widget applies the same rule.
- **Setup checks the starting figure.** The last screen registers the day with the locks held (at most two hours, in case setup is left), waits ten seconds for the phone's reports, and shows Plainlimit's figure inside the iPhone's whole day (see "The last screen shows a part of the whole"; up to build 141, beside the phone's own figure for the counted apps). "Start counting" lets the locks act; "Count from now" starts at zero from that moment, for when the phone misreported. This is the one registration with nothing known yet to guard it.
- **Today's figure is the phone's own.** The report extension draws the day's total for the counted apps to the minute ("Total" report, in the app's display face, which the build copies into the extension). The app's stepped figure fills the battery and shows until the phone has drawn, or where it cannot (after "Count from now", whose report would go by whole hours).
- **What counts can't change while the battery is empty**, like the goal: unticking an app would unlock it.

Build 48 on the phone (7:27 PM, reinstalled, goal 3:00): setup showed the phone's figure 3:10 beside Plainlimit's 1:50. Plainlimit was right. The phone's figure was the report's per-hour total, which is all screen-on time whatever is ticked: the Settings app's day was 3h 29m, but its categories added up to only about 2h 04m, and about 1h 50m of that came after 6 AM. The checkpoints for the day so far arrived within three seconds of registering (cells 1 to 3, and the finer steps to 1:50), so counting from the recharge works. From build 49 the total is added up app by app, like the "What used it" list. The phone took a few seconds to draw the figure on Today (it showed Plainlimit's own 1:50 at first, then 3:18).

## Tidy-up after 1.0.0 (2026-11-17, not in build 75)

The same behaviour as build 75, with the report rules in one place, so a fix for one iOS quirk cannot quietly break another (the build 72 bug was one guard breaking the counting of late reports).

No version number yet: nothing saved needs converting. ## Raising the goal waits for the recharge (the owner, 2026-11-21)
- The owner found a free way round the battery: nearly out, nudge the daily goal up, carry on, and nudge it back later. The settings lock only closed this once the battery was empty, not on the last cell. The rule now (`src/domain/goal.ts`):
  - **Lowering is at once** (being stricter can't be used to cheat), and **going back up as far as the goal the day started with** is at once too, to undo a lowering.
  - **Higher than that waits for the next recharge** (6 AM, or a Pro recharge time). Today keeps its goal and Settings shows both: the stepper has the goal chosen, and under it "Today 3:00 → From 6:00 AM 4:00".
  - **Setup's own day is free:** during setup and until the first recharge after it, every change is at once, to get a feel for it (`day.firstDay`, set as setup finishes).
  - **Lowering stops at the first step above what is already used.** One tap too many would otherwise empty the battery, which locks the settings until the recharge, and the lowering could not be undone. The button looks closed there, and at the goal's limits.
- Kept: `settings.goalMinutes` is today's goal, so the battery, the phone's checkpoints, the covering screens and the widget read it as before. A waiting goal is `settings.nextGoalMinutes`; the goal the day started with is `day.startGoalMinutes` (a copy saved before this reads as today's goal). A finished day keeps the goal it had.
- Moving the recharge time (Pro) also starts a new battery, but it isn't a recharge: a waiting goal goes on waiting, and the day's start and first day carry over (`rechargePassed`: a real recharge is a day on from the battery's own start).
- **The phone:** its checkpoints are fixed to one goal, and only the app can register the day again. So when a recharge starts a waiting goal, the first look at the app after it (opening it, or the app being open at the recharge) registers the day with the new goal and takes off any cover the old goal put up since (put straight back if the battery is empty under the new goal too). Until the app is opened the phone holds to the old, lower goal: the stricter one. Reports are read with the goal their round was registered with (`registeredGoal`, as the widget already did), so the minutes are right in between.
- Going back up after a lowering takes off the last cell's screen the lower goal may have put up; the phone puts it back if the day is past the last cell of the goal it now has.
- Not yet tried on a phone: (1) raise the goal at night, open Plainlimit after the recharge, and see the new goal on Today, the widget and the locks; (2) lower, then go back up, with the last cell's screen showing; (3) a new install's first day changes freely, and the next day doesn't. Test builds' "Start the day over" starts a waiting goal, as a recharge would, to try (1) without waiting for 6 AM.

## A new recharge time waits for the battery to end (2026-11-21)
- The same hole as the goal, for Pro: moving the recharge time to just before now started a new, nearly full battery (the phone counts a day from the last time its recharge time came round). - A battery can only start where its recharge time comes round, so the switch takes the shorter way over:
  - **Earlier** (say 6:00 to 4:00 AM): one more recharge at 6:00 as planned, and that battery counts from 4:00, so the two overlap by two hours. Stricter, never looser. Settings: "One more recharge at 6:00 AM, then at 4:00 AM."

To check on a phone (free, or "Act as the free version"): Settings > Plainlimit Pro opens on Second thought; swipe through all five; tap a tile and it goes there; Recharge time, Hard sums and "Write your own" each open on their own card; the widget's large size opens on the widget card.

## Reports faster than the clock lock nothing (2026-11-24, build 150)

The owner's battery went empty at 2:27 PM with 1:15 of a 2:00 goal used (the phone's own report on Today said 1:15). Build 149's readout: cell-1 at 10:22:44 AM, cell-2 at 10:44:21, cell-3 (60 min) at 2:11:12 PM, then empty (120) at 2:27:49, final-warning (113) at 2:27:50 and cell-5 (100) at 2:27:51, and cell-4 (80) only at 2:34:22. Sixty minutes of use in sixteen minutes cannot happen. The phone was on its charger: iOS 26 fires checkpoints for nothing at times, often when a phone is picked up or plugged in (Apple forum threads 809410 and 814559). The guard after a registration could not catch it, as the day had been registered the evening before; judged from the day's start alone, every one of them could be true.

The rule (`keepsPace` in `src/domain/checkpoints.ts`): a report for M minutes cannot come sooner than M - L minutes after an earlier report for L, less twenty minutes for a slow phone (`PACE_SLACK_MINUTES`). Reports made in the ten minutes after a registration are left out of it, because the phone re-reports the day then with fresh times. A report that came early only pulls the bar earlier, so it can never make a later real one look too soon; only a report that came more than twenty minutes late could, and then nothing locks until the app counts again (failing open, as before). The same rule in all three places:
- **The phone's locks:** each checkpoint's actions carry a `pace` rule (for each lower checkpoint, how long after its report they may act), checked by the monitor extension in the library patch (`plainlimitTooSoon` in `patches/react-native-device-activity+0.6.1.patch`). When it is broken, the actions are skipped and one notification goes instead ("Wrong reading caught", `TOO_SOON_NOTICE`, one id however many checkpoints came at once).
- **The app's count:** `countDay` and `doubtful` pace every report against the day's earlier ones, the minute checkpoints included (`marksOf`). A burst is doubtful, so the day is "miscounted".
- **The widget:** the plan saved for it now lists every checkpoint's minutes and the registration time (`marks`, `registeredAt`), and `reached` paces too. A plan saved before this gets them on the next launch (`sharePaceWithWidget`).

After a miscount the app now counts again by itself (forgets the doubtful reports and registers the day), once a battery, while the battery is not empty by its own count (an empty one is locked for real). After that, Today offers Count again as before. The readout lists the minute checkpoints too, in the order they came. Tests replay 24 November (`reports.test.ts`), including the extension's check. To check on a phone: nothing to do on purpose; on a day the phone sends a burst, nothing locks early, the notification comes, and opening Plainlimit shows the right battery with no "Not counting" box.

## An emergency pass opens the goal too (the owner, 2026-11-24, after build 153)

The owner was locked out and needed Spotify, kept doing sums, and in the end deleted the app to make Spotify an essential. They asked whether a pass lets you change your essentials, and said it should, and probably let you raise the daily goal too: "There's a little bit of cheese there, but there's also the guilt of ruining their streak, and they can only use one pass per day, and they only accrue one per week." (That day's pass had already been used, so it could not have helped that evening.)

How it stood: a pass already opened Settings and Essentials for its 15 minutes (`settingsLocked`), and the pass screen said "make an app an essential, or change your goal". But a higher goal still waited for the recharge (the goal rule), and the phone side had two holes for any change made during a pass:
- **Registering the day again re-reports the checkpoints already passed**, and the empty one covered the apps at once, in the middle of the pass.
- **The pass's end covers the apps by the clock** (the earned-time timer), even when the change had put charge back in the battery (a higher goal, or a new essential whose minutes stop counting). Nothing took that cover off until the recharge.

Now:
- **A higher goal starts today while a pass runs** (`withGoal(..., passOn)` in `src/domain/goal.ts`) and becomes the day's own start, so going down and back up after the pass stays today's. After the pass, the usual rule again. Settings says so in the goal card ("Emergency pass: more time starts today", until when).
- **Nothing covers an app before earned time or a pass ends** (`earnedUntil`, saved by `grantExtraTime`; every checkpoint's "never before" time waits for it, as it does for setup's held locks). The timer at the end covers the apps as before.
- **When a higher goal gives a battery that outlasts the pass's open time** (even with the apps in use all along), the open time ends there and the battery takes over (`batteryTakesOver` in the store, `endExtraTime`): the timer is stopped without covering, and the day is registered with the new goal. Today then says "Emergency pass: settings are open until ..." instead of "everything is open". A smaller raise lets the pass run on.
- **Earned time or a pass that ends with charge in the battery takes its cover off**, once, when the app next looks (`uncoverWithCharge`). That covers new essentials during a pass and a smaller raise. Until the app is opened after the pass, the apps stay covered.
- Stopping the earned-time timer used to report its end, which covers the apps; it is now told to do nothing first (`stopEarnedTimer`), also when counting is turned off or started over.

To check on a phone (needs an empty battery and a pass): (1) use the pass, go to Settings, raise the goal: it changes today, with the pass note; (2) raised well past what is used, the apps stay open after the pass's 15 minutes and lock when the new battery runs out; (3) make a counted app an essential during a pass: the other apps stay open to the pass's end, and the new essential stays open after it.
