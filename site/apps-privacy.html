This policy covers the apps Game Quarry makes in its studio: {{apps_sentence}}, on iPhone, and any other Game Quarry app that links to this page. In short: the apps have no accounts and no servers of ours. What you put in them stays on your iPhone, and we never see it. The only thing that leaves your phone is what the App Store needs to sell you an app's one-time upgrade, where it has one, and bring it back later.

What stays on your iPhone

Everything you enter (items, jobs, photos, receipts, videos, documents, readings, costs, notes, goals and settings) is stored only in the app's own storage on your device. It is not uploaded, we don't receive it, and we couldn't read it if we wanted to. Deleting the app deletes it.

If you back up your iPhone with iCloud or to a computer, that backup may include the app's data, as it does for any app. That copy is yours, under your Apple Account.

The camera, the microphone and your photos

When you add a picture, the app asks to use the camera, or shows you the system photo picker so it only gets the photos you choose. The pictures are copied into the app's own storage and stay there.

An app that films a video (such as a walk-through of a room) asks to use the microphone too, so the video has sound, and only while you are filming. The video is kept in the app's own storage like a photo.

An app that reads text from a picture (a serial number on a label, the shop and price on a receipt) does it on your iPhone, with Apple's own text recognition. The picture and the text are not sent anywhere.

Screen Time

An app that limits your screen time, such as Plainlimit, asks for Screen Time access with Apple's own sheet. From then on Apple's Screen Time, on your iPhone, counts the time spent in the apps you pick and covers them when your limit is reached. The app is told how much of your limit has gone. Apple gives it no list of which apps you opened, when, or for how long, and the apps you pick are kept as codes from Apple that the app can't turn into names.

There is one name the app does learn. When one of its lock screens covers an app, it notes which app that was, so that it can take you straight back there once you are let in. It keeps only the latest one, replaces it the next time, and keeps it on your iPhone: it is not sent to us or to anyone else.

The day's total and the list of apps that used the most time are drawn on your screen by Apple's Screen Time, in a part of the app that Apple keeps from reading them back or sending anything out. A Home Screen widget shows the same battery, from what the app keeps on your iPhone. Nothing about your screen time is sent to us or to anyone else. Turning the app's access off in the Settings app (under Screen Time), turning it off in the app, or deleting the app stops it and takes its locks away.

Reminders

An app that reminds you of something, or tells you your screen time is nearly out, schedules its notifications on your iPhone itself, as local notifications, and only after you turn them on. They are not sent from a server and no push service is involved: the reminder and what it says never leave the phone.

Reports, spreadsheets and backups

A report, a spreadsheet or a backup file is made on your iPhone and handed to the share sheet. Where it goes from there (Files, Mail, AirDrop, a printer, your insurer) is your choice. Once you send a file to someone or save it to a service, their own policy covers it.

An app that can back itself up does so only after you turn it on and choose a folder, such as one in iCloud Drive. The backup is saved into that folder on your iPhone, and iCloud Drive keeps it under your Apple Account, as it does any file you save there. It does not come to us. Turning it off stops it; the files already in the folder stay yours to keep or delete.

Purchases (Apple)

Where an app has an optional purchase, it is paid once. Payment is handled entirely by the App Store: we never see your card or payment details. The app asks the App Store on your iPhone whether your Apple Account holds the purchase, which is also how it comes back on a new iPhone. No other company is involved, and the app sends nothing to us or to anyone else. Apple keeps the record of the purchase under your Apple Account, under Apple's privacy policy.

What we don't do

  • We don't ask for your name, your email address or any account.
  • The apps show no ads and contain no advertising or analytics code.
  • We don't sell or share personal data, because we don't have any.
  • The apps are made for adults and are not aimed at children.

The web previews

The test versions on this site run in your browser and keep what you enter in that browser's storage for this site. Nothing is sent to us. Clearing the site's data removes it. They sell nothing.

Your choices

You can delete everything an app holds by deleting the app, or with the erase option where an app has one. The record of a purchase is kept by Apple under your Apple Account, not by us.
